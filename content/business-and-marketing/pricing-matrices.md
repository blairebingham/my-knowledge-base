# Mathematical Service Pricing Matrices

## Calculating Your Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) 

Many independent lash artists fail within their first two years because they price their services based entirely on what local competitors are charging, rather than calculating their own actual operational costs. To build a highly profitable business model, you must mathematically determine your precise hourly overhead expenses and your exact Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) per client application.

### The Pricing Calculation Formula 

To establish an accurate, sustainable price tier for your full sets and touch-up appointments, factor in the following business metrics:

1. **Fixed Overhead Costs:** Add up your monthly commercial studio rent, business insurance, booking software fees, and utility bills. Divide this total by the number of hours you intend to work each month. 
2. **Variable Supply Costs:** Calculate the exact cost of consumable items used during a single service, including eye patches, mascara wands, adhesive drops, and microswabs. 
3. **The Hourly Labor Rate:** Decide on a sustainable, professional hourly wage for yourself based on your specific training and local market demand.

## Factoring in Environmental Degradation 

When building your pricing matrix, remember to account for supply waste caused by shelf-life limits. For example, because open bottles of glue degrade quickly outside the tight environmental boundaries detailed in [[products-and-tools/humidity-and-temperature|humidity and temperature parameters]], you must replace your adhesive bottle every four weeks. Factor the cost of a fresh monthly bottle directly into your recurring supply overhead.
