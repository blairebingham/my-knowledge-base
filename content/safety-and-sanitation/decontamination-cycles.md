# The Three Levels of Decontamination

## Maintaining Medical-Grade Hygiene 

In the professional lash suite, protecting the client's eye health requires a strict commitment to proper sanitation protocols. Because tools come into direct contact with the sensitive eyelid margin, artists must understand the three distinct levels of decontamination recognized by state cosmetology boards: cleaning, disinfecting, and sterilizing. 

### Breakdown of the Sanitation Process

To eliminate the risk of cross-contamination, non-porous tools must undergo a rigorous multi-step cleaning process between every single client appointment: 

1. **Cleaning (Sanitization):** The physical removal of visible debris, adhesive residue, and oil using warm water, antimicrobial soap, and a friction brush. This lowers the pathogen load but does not kill viruses or bacteria. 
2. **Disinfection:** Submerging completely dried tools into an approved hospital-grade EPA disinfectant bath to destroy bacteria, fungi, and viruses. 
3. **Sterilization:** The highest tier of decontamination, which completely eradicates all microbial life, including highly resistant bacterial spores, usually achieved via an autoclave or dry heat sterilizer.

> **Mandatory Rule:** Any porous item—such as eye pads, mascara wands, microswabs, or paper tape—cannot be decontaminated. They must be discarded immediately in a closed trash bin after a single use.

## Protecting Your Inventory Investment 

Proper decontamination protects both your clients and your physical tools.
