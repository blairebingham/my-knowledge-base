# Chemical Disinfection & Mixing Ratios

## Safe Handling of Hospital-Grade Disinfectants 

To legally operate a beauty workspace, a technician must maintain an approved liquid disinfectant station. The industry standard for tool immersion is **Barbicide** or similar quaternary ammonium compounds ("quats"). Because these products are sold as concentrated liquids, lash artists must mathematically compute mixing ratios to ensure the solution is strong enough to kill pathogens without damaging instruments or causing chemical skin burns. 

### Calculating the Perfect Ratio 

An ineffective, under-concentrated mix creates a biohazard, while an over-concentrated mix corrodes premium steel. Follow these strict manufacturing standards: 

* **The Standard Mix:** Combine 2 ounces (4 tablespoons) of Barbicide concentrate into 32 ounces (4 cups) of cold tap water. 
* **The Contact Time:** Total immersion is required for exactly 10 minutes. Removing tools early leaves pathogens alive. Leaving them overnight rusts the tips. 
* **The Replacement Cycle:** The solution must be completely dumped, washed, and remade daily, or sooner if visible debris or cloudiness appears.

## Environmental Safety and Fume Control 

Chemical safety extends beyond the immersion jar. High-strength disinfectants and liquid cyanoacrylate glues release chemical vapors. Artists should track workspace ventilation to prevent these fumes from compounding within the tight humidity boundaries outlined in [[products-and-tools/humidity-and-temperature|humidity and temperature control limits]].
