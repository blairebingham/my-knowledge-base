# Bloodborne Pathogens & Exposure Control

## Managing Occupational Exposure Risks 

Although eyelash extension application is categorized as a non-invasive cosmetic service, working with sharp, pointed tweezers near thin eyelid tissues creates an inherent risk of accidental skin punctures. Because of this, lash artists must be fully trained in bloodborne pathogen (BBP) mitigation to handle accidental exposures to microscopic traces of human blood or bodily fluids safely.

### Primary Pathogens of Concern 

Technicians must maintain strict universal precautions to guard against highly resilient viruses that can survive on unsterilized surfaces for days: 

* **Hepatitis B (HBV) and Hepatitis C (HCV):** Highly contagious viral infections that cause severe liver damage and can easily spread via micro-tears or puncture wounds. 
* **Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV):** The virus responsible for attacking the human immune system, requiring rapid exposure control protocols if an accidental stick occurs.

## Executing an Exposure Incident Protocol 

If a sharp tweezer tip accidentally punctures a client or the artist's skin, stop the service immediately. Wash the area thoroughly with soap and water, apply an antiseptic, seal the wound with a waterproof bandage, and completely isolate all contaminated tools. These contaminated tools must be taken out of service immediately until they can undergo the full multi-step cleaning loop detailed in [[safety-and-sanitization/decontamination-cycles|the three levels of decontamination]].
