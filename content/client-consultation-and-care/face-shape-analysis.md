# Structural Face and Eye Shape Analysis

## Assessing Ocular Topography 

A generic, one-size-fits-all approach to lash extensions inevitably leads to unflattering sets that can make a client's eyes appear tired, droopy, or unnaturally wide. Before touching a single lash, an artist must perform a comprehensive structural facial analysis to evaluate bone structure, eye plane, and natural lid orientation.

### Key Ocular Attributes to Evaluate 

During your physical assessment, categorize the client's eyes based on three primary structural axes: 

* **Eye Distance:** Measure whether the eyes are close-set (closer together than one eye's width) or wide-set. Close-set eyes benefit from length concentrated on the outer corners to draw the gaze outward. 
* **Eye Plane:** Identify if the outer corners tilt upward (exotic/cat-like) or slope downward (droopy). Downward-sloping eyes require a lifted curl on the outer edges to counteract a tired expression. 
* **Lid Depth:** Determine if the eyes are deep-set, prominent, or hooded. Hooded lids require specialized L or M curls to clear the heavy brow skin fold cleanly.

> "A master stylist does not copy trends. They balance the natural asymmetry of the human face using strategic lengths and weights."

## Translating Strategy to the Eye Pad 

Once you have evaluated these structural factors, draw your game plan directly onto the under-eye gel patches using the techniques outlined in our guide to [[application-techniques/lash-mapping-styles|lash mapping and styling frameworks]]. Matching the styling map directly to facial topography ensures the set enhances, rather than overpowers, the client's features.
