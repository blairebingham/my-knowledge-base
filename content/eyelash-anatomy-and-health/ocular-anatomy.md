# Ocular Anatomy & Eyelid Physiology 

## The Physical Structure of the Eye Area

The human eyelid, or palpebra, serves as a primary protective barrier for the ocular globe. For a lash tech, a deep understanding of the delicate muscular, glandular, and dermal structures surrounding the lash line is crucial to avoid severe chemical and mechanical injuries during a service. 

### Key Glandular Structures to Monitor 

The margin of the eyelid houses specialized glands that dictate the health and cleanliness of the eye environment: 

* **Meibomian Glands:** Located inside the tarsal plate, these sebaceous glands secrete lipids that form the top layer of the tear film, preventing evaporation. If extensions are placed too close to the lid skin, they can block these glands, leading to dry eye or painful styes. 
* **Glands of Zeis and Moll:** These smaller sweat and oil glands open directly into the hair follicles of the eyelashes. Excess oil buildup here can harbor bacteria or cause extensions to lose their bond with our chosen [[02-products-and-tools/adhesive-chemistry|adhesive chemicals]]. 

## The Eyelid Margin Safety Window 

The absolute baseline rule of safe extension application dictates that an extension must never touch the skin of the eyelid. Extensions must be placed exactly 0.5mm to 1.0mm away from the skin margin. Touching the skin leads to chronic mechanical friction, localized swelling, and severe inflammation of the eyelid margin, a condition known medically as blepharitis.
