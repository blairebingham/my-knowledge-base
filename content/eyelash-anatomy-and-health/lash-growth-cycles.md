# The Trichology of Eyelash Growth Cycles
## The Three Phases of Lash Development

To apply eyelash extensions safely without causing permanent follicular damage, an artist must understand the biological hair growth cycle, known as trichology. Unlike scalp hair, which can grow for years, natural eyelashes have a significantly shorter life cycle, typically lasting between 60 to 90 days. This cycle is continuously divided into three distinct development phases acting independently across the lid line. 

### 1. The Anagen Phase (Active Growth)

The Anagen phase represents the active growth period where the lash follicle is connected directly to the dermal papilla blood supply. Approximately 35-40% of the upper eyelashes are in this phase at any given time. These lashes are short, fine, and highly delicate. 

> **Critical Application Rule:** Applying heavy extension fibers to an active Anagen lash will weigh down the root, causing premature shedding or permanent damage to the hair follicle.


### 2. The Catagen Phase (Transition)

During the Catagen phase, active growth stops. The hair follicle shrinks, detaches from the blood supply, and enters a transitional stage lasting roughly two to three weeks. The lash has reached its maximum natural length and is resting. 

### 3. The Telogen Phase (Resting & Shedding)

The telogen phase is the final resting state where the hair sits completely dormant in the follicle for roughly 4 to 8 weeks before naturally shedding to make room for a new hair bulb. 

## Structural Implications for Lash Artists 

Understanding these phases allows a technician to evaluate natural lash health during a baseline [[05-client-consultation-and-care/index|client consultation profile]]. Recognizing baby Anagen lashes ensures you avoid overloading them with heavy extensions, directly protecting the client from long-term follicle damage.
