# Lash Adhesive Chemistry & Curing
## Understanding Cyanoacrylate

The backbone of any professional eyelash extension adhesive is **cyanoacrylate**. Unlike standard glues that dry via evaporation, lash extensions rely on a chemical reaction called polymerization. When exposed to ambient moisture in the air, the liquid monomers rapidly cross-link into a solid acrylic plastic chain. 
### The Role Of Stabilizers and Carbon Black

To keep the adhesive liquid inside the bottle, manufacturers add acidic stabilizers. Once poured onto a stone or into a glue ring, moisture neutralizes these stabilizers, initiating the curing process. **Carbon black** is typically added to provide a deep black pigment that blends seamlessly with dark natural eyelashes. 

> "Adhesive behavior changes instantly with environmental shifts. A technician who does not track humidity is guessing, not lashing." 
## Environmental Impact on Curing 

Because moisture acts as the catalyst, your workspace environment is critical. If your room is too dry, the glue will cure too slowly, leading to poor retention and sticky lashes. For optimal performance, pair your adhesive knowledge with proper [[humidity-and-temperature|environmental tracking controls]]. Proper curing directly ensures the long-term retention of your [[lash-extension-types|extension selection choices]].
